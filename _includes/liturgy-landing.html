What Are Sacraments?

Sacraments are special celebrations where God meets us and blesses us. In the Syriac language, they are called rozo—which means a hidden treasure or mystery that God reveals. They're not just reminders of God—they're real moments when God's love and power actually work in our lives and change our hearts. In our church, we have seven special mysteries that mark important moments as we grow up: when we're baptized as babies, when we receive the Holy Spirit, when we receive communion with Jesus, when we say sorry for mistakes, when we need healing, when we get married, and when people become priests. Each one is God's way of saying, "I love you. I'm here with you at every important moment of your life."

Apostolic & Historical Continuity

The Malankara Orthodox Church's sacramental practice traces directly to the Antiochene liturgical tradition, one of the oldest continuous Christian liturgies. In 1665, under the guidance of Mar Gregorios of Jerusalem, the Church formally adopted Syrian Orthodox liturgical practices, establishing these sacred traditions observed today.

Our primary Eucharistic prayer, the Anaphora of St. James, originates from the 5th century in Greek and was translated into Syriac, maintaining unbroken apostolic succession from the first bishop of Jerusalem and connecting us directly to the apostles and the early Church.
