What Are Sacraments?

Sacraments are far more than symbolic rituals—they are sacred celebrations of divine mysteries where the invisible realities of God's grace become tangibly present in the Church. The Syriac word rozo (mystery) captures this perfectly: it means not merely to symbolize, but to celebrate and make present the redemptive work of Christ. In the Syrian Orthodox tradition, sacraments are understood as divino-human actions—moments where God's infinite grace meets human nature and transforms it. They are not isolated acts performed by individuals, but rather the corporate liturgical actions of the Church's body, through which Christ incorporates us into His death and resurrection.

Unlike viewing sacraments as magical formulas or mechanical channels of grace, the Eastern Orthodox approach recognizes them as celebrations of the mystery of Christ's death, resurrection, and glorification. Through sacraments, we do not merely remember these redemptive acts—we participate in them, entering into the living presence of Christ's transformative power. The seven principal mysteries mark crucial transitions in the believer's journey, sanctifying every stage of human existence from birth to eternity. Yet the Church recognizes that sacramentality extends throughout the entire life of faith—God's grace is not confined to formal sacraments alone, but flows through all the Church's sacred actions.

Apostolic & Historical Continuity

The Malankara Orthodox Church's sacramental practice traces directly to the Antiochene liturgical tradition, one of the oldest continuous Christian liturgies. In 1665, under the guidance of Mar Gregorios of Jerusalem, the Church formally adopted Syrian Orthodox liturgical practices, establishing these sacred traditions observed today.

Our primary Eucharistic prayer, the Anaphora of St. James, originates from the 5th century in Greek and was translated into Syriac, maintaining unbroken apostolic succession from the first bishop of Jerusalem and connecting us directly to the apostles and the early Church.
